eCTHP is INE's practical threat-hunting certification: simulated enterprise environments where the job isn't responding to an alert, it's finding the compromise before one ever fires.

## What I learned

- **Threat-hunting methodology**: building and testing a hypothesis instead of waiting for a detection rule to catch something.
- **Cyber Threat Intelligence as an input**: using MITRE ATT&CK and known adversary behavior to decide where to hunt first, not hunting blind.
- **Network-based hunting**: digging through traffic with Wireshark to spot C2, beaconing and lateral movement that blends into normal noise.
- **Endpoint-based hunting**: using host telemetry and log data (Splunk/ELK) to surface anomalies in process, auth and file activity.
- **Turning a hunch into evidence**: documenting a hunt so it either closes clean or hands off a confirmed incident.

## Why it matters to my work

This fills the gap between my CTI background and eCIR: CTI tells me what to look for, eCIR is what happens once something's confirmed, and threat hunting is the proactive middle step — going looking before the SOC's tools tell me to. It's the certification that maps most directly onto turning alert fatigue into actually getting ahead of an intrusion.
