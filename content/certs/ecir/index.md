eCIR is INE's practical incident-response certification. Instead of multiple-choice theory, you're handed a compromised environment and have to work it the way a real IR engagement goes: detect, scope, contain, and explain what happened.

## What I learned

- **Triage under time pressure**: deciding what to look at first when you don't yet know what the incident is.
- **Host-based forensics**: pulling evidence from memory, the filesystem and the registry to reconstruct attacker activity on Windows and Linux hosts.
- **Network traffic analysis**: tying host artifacts back to PCAP evidence to confirm C2 and lateral movement.
- **Log analysis and timeline building**: correlating scattered events into a single, defensible incident timeline.
- **Writing it up**: turning a technical investigation into a clear incident report a non-technical stakeholder can act on.

## Why it matters to my work

This is the closest a certification gets to my actual day job. I triage and close alerts in a SOC every shift, and eCIR is the same muscle: take ambiguous evidence, figure out what really happened, and be able to defend the conclusion. It also sharpened the forensics side of writeups like my [Operation GhostVein](/writeups/operation-ghostvein/) piece.
